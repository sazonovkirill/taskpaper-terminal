tpt · the infinite outliner for your terminal

Think in outlines.
Stay in the terminal.

tpt is a keyboard-driven infinite outliner that keeps everything in one plain TaskPaper file you own.

cargo install --git https://github.com/sazonovkirill/taskpaper-terminal

Read the Tutorial View on GitHub

Plain text

Your outline is a text file.

Everything lives in one .taskpaper file: readable in any editor, easy to sync, diff and back up, and open in TaskPaper too. Tags like @due and @done stay in the file and out of your way.

Infinite outline

Zoom into anything.

Every block can hold more blocks, as deep as you like. Zoom into one and it becomes the whole screen, with breadcrumbs to find your way back. Fold what you don't need right now.

. zoom in , zoom out h l fold and unfold

Keyboard first

Move like vim. Type like a notebook.

In navigate mode the keys move, fold, indent and rearrange whole blocks. Press Enter to type into one, Esc to step back out. Every command has a key that works in any terminal, kitty keyboard protocol or not.

j k move o new block Tab indent x done z undo

Quick Add

Catch the thought. Keep your place.

Quick Add drops a task into your inbox without leaving where you are. Say "tomorrow" or "next Friday" and it becomes a date.

q add to the inbox

Dates and views

Today, gathered for you.

Start and due dates read as "today", "Tue" or "yesterday". The Today view gathers what needs you today from across the whole outline, with the path to each. Upcoming, Overdue, Logbook, Starred, Flagged and Colors are a keystroke away.

Navigator

Everywhere is close.

The Navigator lists your inbox, the special views, bookmarks and every project with its open tasks. Type to filter, press Enter to go. Finders jump to any block or command by name.

Ctrl+G Navigator Ctrl+K go to block Ctrl+P commands

Notes and comments

Details, out of the way.

Give any block a Markdown note and dated comments. In the outline they're a quiet icon; open the panel to read and write them. In the file they're ordinary indented lines, so nothing is lost.

\ notes and comments c add a comment

Panes

Two places at once. Or ten.

Split the screen into as many panes as fit, each zoomed into its own part of the same file. Edit in one and the others follow.

Ctrl+W v open the focused block in a new pane

Make it yours

Twelve themes. A little weather.

Pick from twelve built-in themes, or keep Terminal Dark, which takes its colors from your terminal. Gentle animations show where things land. Step away for half an hour and the season's weather drifts across the screen.

Anywhere

Wherever there's a terminal.

  • SSH and tmuxRuns on a server as well as on your laptop.
  • macOS and LinuxiTerm2, Terminal.app, Ghostty, kitty, WezTerm, Alacritty, GNOME Terminal.
  • Saves as you goEvery change is written to the file, with a daily backup.
  • Edit it anywhereChange the file in another app and tpt picks it up.
  • FastBuilt to open a 100,000-line outline in under a third of a second.
  • No accountNo sign-in, no cloud, no telemetry.
