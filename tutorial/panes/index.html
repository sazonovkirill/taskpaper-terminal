Panes

On this page

  • Open a pane
  • Move between panes
  • What each pane keeps
  • Close a pane

To see two places at once, split the screen into panes, side by side.

Open a pane🔗

Go to “Trip to Lisbon” and press Ctrl+W v (Ctrl+W, then v). g | does the same. A second pane opens on the right, zoomed into the trip. The left pane still shows the whole outline. Each pane’s header ends in its number, 1 and 2.

The new pane has the keys, and only its focus bar shows.

Move between panes🔗

  • g 1 and g 2 focus pane 1 and pane 2. Alt+1 and Alt+2 do the same, where your terminal lets Alt through.
  • Ctrl+W h and Ctrl+W l focus the pane to the left and right. In terminals with the kitty keyboard protocol, Ctrl+, and Ctrl+. do too.

Try it: press g 1, go to “Look up tram pass prices” in “Before we go” and press x. It’s checked off in both panes: they’re two views of one file.

What each pane keeps🔗

Each pane has its own zoom, history and show-done setting. Folds are shared between panes.

Ctrl+W s opens the pane’s current place, Today included, in a new pane. In the Navigator and Go to Symbol, Ctrl+T opens the choice in a new pane.

Close a pane🔗

Press Ctrl+W c to close the focused pane. The last pane can’t be closed.

tpt remembers your panes: quit, open the file again, and they’re back as you left them. The Reference has the keys for resizing and reordering panes.
