Shape the outline

Indent and outdent🔗

Under “Home”, “Buy a new washer” is really part of “Fix the dripping kitchen tap”.

Move to “Buy a new washer” and press Tab. It becomes a subtask of the tap. Shift+Tab moves a line back out. If you know vim, > and < do the same.

A line always takes everything inside it along.

Move lines up and down🔗

In “Packing”, move to “Comfortable shoes” and press K (that’s Shift+k) twice. The shoes move to the top of the list. J moves a line down. Alt+k and Alt+j do the same.

On a Mac, Alt is Option, and your terminal must send it: turn on “Use Option as Meta key” in Terminal, or set Option to “Esc+” in iTerm2. Move Up and Move Down are also in the command palette (last chapter).

Fold🔗

Move to “Trip to Lisbon” and press h. The project folds up into one line, ending in ▸ 9: nine open tasks are hidden inside. Press l to unfold it.

On a folded line, or one with nothing inside, h goes to the parent instead. ’ just toggles the fold.

Press _ to fold everything and + to unfold it all. A file always opens unfolded.

Add a project🔗

Press G to go to the last line, then # to go to its parent, “Reading list”. Now press o to add a line below the reading list. Next to a project, a new line starts as plain text. Type “Ideas:” and press Esc: the colon at the end makes it a project.

Now press i to add a line inside the project, as its last child. Inside a project, a new line is a task, with its dash already there. Type “Learn to juggle” and press Esc.

The keys that add lines pick a place: o below, O above, i as the last child and I as the first, a at the end of the list and A at its start. The new line is a task next to a task or inside a project or task, and text anywhere else. Typing changes the type: end a line with a colon to make a project, start it with “- “ to make a task, or press Backspace at the start of a task to delete its dash.

Delete🔗

To cut a line and everything in it, press d d, as in vim; Delete deletes it without copying. Try it on “Learn to juggle”, then press z or u to bring it back.

Copy, paste, selection and the rest are in the Reference.
