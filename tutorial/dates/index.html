Dates and Today

On this page

  • Quick dates
  • Any date
  • The Today view

A start date says when you want to begin something, a due date when it has to be done. Both show as chips at the right end of the row.

Quick dates🔗

Go to “Reply to Sam about Saturday” in the INBOX and press T. A red chip appears: due today. Now try the others:

  • On “Buy a birthday card for Mum”, press W: due tomorrow.
  • On “Look up tram pass prices”, press t: a green chip, starting today.
  • On “Call the dentist”, under “Home”, press w: starting tomorrow.

Lowercase sets the start date, uppercase the due date. ] and [ move the start date a day later or earlier, } and { the due date. Chips name dates as you’d say them, like tomorrow or Fri, and a past due date turns into a bold overdue chip.

Any date🔗

For any other date, press m d for the due date or m s for the start date. Go to “Book the hotel”, press m d and type fri. The panel at the bottom shows the date it understood. Press Enter to set it, or Esc to cancel.

The prompt reads words like tomorrow, fri or next mon, offsets like +3d or 2 weeks, and dates like 2026-05-02. To remove a date, erase the text and press Enter.

The Today view🔗

Now press g t. The pane switches to Today: a flat list of everything that starts or is due today or earlier, from anywhere in the file. You should see “Reply to Sam about Saturday” and “Look up tram pass prices”, and also “Tram 28, early in the morning”, because starred lines show up in Today too. Each line shows the path to where it lives.

These aren’t copies: x here checks the task off in the outline too, and . jumps to a line’s place in the outline.

Press g U for Upcoming, the next seven days grouped by day: the card and the dentist under tomorrow, the hotel under its day. H goes back, as after a zoom.

The next chapter shows where to find the other views. The Reference lists every date you can type.
