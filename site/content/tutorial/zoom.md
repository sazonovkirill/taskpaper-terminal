## Zoom in

Move to "Trip to Lisbon" and press <kbd>.</kbd> (period). The screen now shows only the trip, with its name as a title.

The breadcrumb bar at the top now reads `⌂ › Trip to Lisbon`, with a house icon for `⌂`: the path from the top of the file to here.

Go one level deeper. Move to "Things to see" and press <kbd>.</kbd> again. The breadcrumbs grow to `⌂ › Trip to Lisbon › Things to see`, and you see three tasks and nothing else.

{{ shot(src="tutorial/zoom", alt="tpt zoomed into Things to see, with the breadcrumb bar reading Trip to Lisbon then Things to see, and three tasks below the title", caption="Zoomed into Things to see. The breadcrumbs show the way back.") }}

You can zoom into any line, even a single task, and everything works the same there. You're still editing the real outline, just one part of it.

## Zoom out

Press <kbd>,</kbd> (the key beside <kbd>.</kbd>) to zoom out one level, back to the trip. Press <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>h</kbd> to go all the way home, to the top of the file.

## Back and forward

Like a web browser, tpt remembers where you've been. Press <kbd>H</kbd> to go back, and <kbd>L</kbd> to go forward. Try it: <kbd>H</kbd> takes you back into the trip.

With a mouse, you can also click a crumb to zoom there.

## Why it matters

Zooming lets one file hold everything without feeling crowded. The trip doesn't need a list of its own: zoom into it while you plan, and out when you're done.

Press <kbd>g</kbd> <kbd>h</kbd> to go home before the next chapter. The other zoom keys are listed in the [Reference](@/reference/_index.md).
